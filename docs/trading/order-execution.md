# Order Execution

Understanding how your orders get filled is essential for trading effectively on Zenex. The protocol uses oracle-based pricing and a keeper network to ensure that all executions happen transparently and at verifiable prices.

## Market Orders

Market orders are the most straightforward way to open a position. When you submit a market order, it executes in the same transaction at the current oracle price. There is no order book, no matching engine, and no waiting. You specify your collateral amount, leverage, direction (long or short), and the market you want to trade. The protocol verifies the oracle price, calculates your entry, applies the opening fees, and creates your position, all atomically in a single transaction.

Because execution is instant, market orders are ideal when you want to enter a position immediately at the prevailing price. The price you receive is the Pyth Lazer oracle price at the time your transaction is processed, which provides a consistent and tamper-resistant reference.

## Limit Orders

Limit orders allow you to set a specific price at which you want to enter the market. When you place a limit order, your collateral is committed to the protocol, but no position is created yet. Instead, the order sits in a pending state until the oracle price reaches your target.

For long positions, your limit order fills when the oracle price drops to or below your specified level. This lets you buy into a dip at a price you consider favorable. For short positions, the limit fills when the oracle price rises to or above your target, letting you enter a short at a higher price. If the market never reaches your target, the order remains pending indefinitely until you cancel it and reclaim your collateral.

Limit orders are not executed by the protocol automatically. They are filled by keepers, which are external bots that continuously monitor oracle prices and on-chain order state. When a keeper detects that the oracle price satisfies a pending limit order's conditions, it submits a transaction to fill that order on the trader's behalf.

## Keeper Execution

Keepers are the engine that drives conditional execution on Zenex. They are permissionless bots that anyone can run, and they perform four critical functions: filling limit orders, executing stop-loss triggers, executing take-profit triggers, and liquidating underwater positions.

When you set a stop-loss on your position, you are defining a price level at which you want the position closed to limit your downside. When you set a take-profit, you define a price at which you want to lock in gains. Neither of these triggers executes on its own. Keepers monitor the oracle price feed and, when a trigger condition is met, submit a transaction to close your position. The same applies to liquidations: keepers identify positions whose equity has fallen below the liquidation threshold and submit liquidation transactions to protect the protocol.

Keepers earn a percentage of the trading fees generated by each action they execute. This percentage is called the caller rate and is set as a global protocol parameter. The economic incentive ensures that there are always bots competing to execute actions as quickly as possible. Faster keepers capture more opportunities, which benefits traders through prompt execution of their orders and risk management settings.

Because keeper execution is permissionless and competitive, multiple keepers may detect the same actionable condition simultaneously. They race to submit their transactions, and the first valid transaction to be included on the ledger wins. The losing keepers' transactions are simply rejected. This competitive dynamic keeps execution latency low and ensures that no single keeper can monopolize the system.

## Batch Processing

For efficiency, keepers can bundle multiple actions into a single transaction. A keeper might fill three limit orders, execute two stop-losses, and process a liquidation all in one call to the protocol's `execute` function. Batching reduces transaction costs for keepers and improves overall throughput for the protocol.

From a trader's perspective, batching is invisible. Your order gets filled in the same way regardless of whether the keeper is processing it alone or alongside a dozen other actions. The key benefit is that batching makes it economically viable for keepers to process even small actions that might not justify a standalone transaction.

## Minimum Open Time

There is one important timing constraint to be aware of. All positions have a minimum open time of 30 seconds. During this window, you cannot close the position yourself, and stop-loss and take-profit triggers will not execute even if their price conditions are met. This prevents strategies that exploit momentary oracle price fluctuations to extract value from the vault.

Liquidation is the one exception. If your position's equity falls below the liquidation threshold, keepers can liquidate it immediately, regardless of how long the position has been open. Protecting the protocol from bad debt takes priority over the minimum open time.
